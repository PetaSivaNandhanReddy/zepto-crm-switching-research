R1 · RECOMMENDED (CONDITIONAL, TESTABLE)

Serviceability Waitlist & Expansion-Triggered Reactivation

Moderate Confidence (Descriptive)

Customer Problem: Coverage-driven involuntary exit and non-adoption (unserved residential localities).

Underlying Evidence: Primary survey: Area is the main exit reason for 31/73 former users (Class A) and the main never-use reason for 37/60; 19/28 coverage-primary leavers state positive return willingness. Secondary filing: Zepto earmarks ₹16,289.75M for dark-store network expansion across FY27–FY29 (UDRHP pp. 143–145).

Target Segment: Residents of unserved localities (former Class A users, n = 31; never-users citing coverage constraints, n = 37).

Operational Mechanism: Serviceability waitlist; expansion-triggered automated reactivation; pin-code-based messaging (CRM interpretation).

Pilot Implementation: Capture pin codes at install/registration and on failed-serviceability screens; trigger an automated notification when a dark store covering that catchment goes live; suppress if already served.

Expected Output: Higher first-order conversion after a coverage launch (to be tested empirically in pilot, not assumed).

Key Limitations: Exit was involuntary, so this represents geographic demand recapture rather than true behavioural retention; item wording ambiguity; stated intention may overstate actual conversion.

Target KPI: Waitlist size; waitlist-to-first-order conversion; 30/90-day repeat rate of reactivated users Source: Table 19 canonical record (Table 13d & UDRHP S01)
R2 · CONDITIONAL (PILOT WITH CONTROL GROUP)

Restock Alerts & Intelligent Substitution Recommendations

Low-Moderate Confidence

Customer Problem: Competitor pull and assortment stock-out gaps causing platform leakage.

Underlying Evidence: Primary survey: Among 32 voluntary-only former users, availability + variety = 16/32 main reasons; 41/68 evaluating former users state rivals carried products not easily found on Zepto; H2/H3 supported on raw scores only. Secondary filing: Store-level unique SKUs expanded from 12,312 to 46,623 (FY26) and 49,602 in Q4 FY26 (UDRHP p. 162, p. 538); in-house ML recommendations engine active (p. 246).

Target Segment: Current users experiencing repeated failed searches or out-of-stock views; active multi-platform users.

Operational Mechanism: Restock/availability alerts; substitution recommendations; pin-code assortment personalisation (CRM interpretation).

Pilot Implementation: Log failed searches and out-of-stock views per user; notify immediately upon restock; rank close substitutes using the existing in-house recommendations engine (UDRHP p. 246 confirms capability).

Expected Output: Fewer abandoned baskets and reduced item leakage to rivals (to be evaluated in a randomized holdout pilot).

Key Limitations: Associational survey evidence; variety not empirically separable from general attractiveness (HTMT 0.94); recommendation engine effectiveness is a proposed test, not an established certainty.

Target KPI: Repeat rate among stock-out-affected users vs control; notify-me conversion; share of regular items bought on Zepto Source: Table 19 canonical record (Table 13e & UDRHP S01)
R3 · CONDITIONAL (SMALL N; MATCHED REASONS ONLY)

Segmented Win-Back for Voluntary Assortment Leavers

Low Confidence (Small n=16)

Customer Problem: Departure of voluntary users driven specifically by catalog availability or product variety limitations.

Underlying Evidence: Primary survey: Voluntary-only former users n = 32; 15/30 state return willingness; main reasons: availability 8, variety 8, delivery 4, app 3, discounts 3. Secondary filing: Cross-category expansion depth reaches 18 product categories at 2 years of tenure (UDRHP p. 252).

Target Segment: Former users whose documented main exit reason was product availability or variety (n = 16).

Operational Mechanism: Reason-specific win-back messaging triggered when the relevant missing product category or brand is introduced (CRM interpretation).

Pilot Implementation: Match the stored exit reason (or inferred search history) to catalog updates; no proactive win-back for minority reasons with counts of 1–3 (insufficient evidence base).

Expected Output: Higher reactivation rate among the matched assortment segment (to be tested against an uncontacted holdout control group).

Key Limitations: Small matched segment (n = 16); stated return intention only; broad-based promotional discount incentives are unsupported (only 3/32 cited discounts).

Target KPI: Reactivation rate vs holdout; 30/90-day repeat purchase rate after reactivation Source: Table 19 canonical record (Table 13e, Availability 8, Variety 8)
R4 · CRITICAL RESEARCH FINDING (UNRECOMMENDED)

Switching-Barrier or Habit Lock-In Mechanisms

Not Recommended on Current Evidence

Canonical Evaluation Status: NOT RECOMMENDED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE — switching-barrier evidence is inconclusive and the current measurement is problematic; re-measure switching barriers with a validated scale before designing lock-in interventions.

Target Problem: Proposed switching-cost or artificial platform lock-in schemes.

Underlying Evidence: Primary survey: Hypothesis H5 yielded an anomalous positive association (ρ = +0.721), which collapses under within-person centering (ρ = +0.054) and exhibits severe discriminant overlap with intention (HTMT 0.92); familiarity (H6) and inertia (H7) were statistically non-significant. Secondary filing: Delivery and merchant partner multi-homing risk disclosures (UDRHP pp. 27–28, 30); Zepto Pass active (p. 258). Academic literature: Mixed switching-cost findings across on-demand studies.

Operational Mechanism: NONE. No lock-in, cancellation friction, or artificial barrier mechanism should be deployed.

Required Research Action: Re-measure switching barriers with a validated, reverse-worded psychometric scale before any lock-in intervention is conceived or designed.

Scientific Conclusion: Attempting to artificially trap users in a competitive quick-commerce market where consumers, delivery partners, and merchants multi-home freely is empirically unsupported and risks accelerating customer alienation.

Status: Table 19 canonical determination Source: Table 12a (H5, H6, H7) & Table 20 (H5 item diagnostics)
R5 · MONITOR ONLY (OPERATIONAL SAFEGUARD)

Trigger-Based Service Recovery Safeguard

Low Confidence (Monitor Only)

Customer Problem: Service delivery experience failures (delayed delivery SLA breaches, app glitches, customer support friction, product quality defects).

Underlying Evidence: Primary survey: Satisfaction was not significantly associated with switching intention (ρ = +0.170, Holm p = 0.2492, inconclusive); service experience reasons are minority exit mentions (delivery 4, app 3 of 32 voluntary exits). Secondary filing: Store densification targets sub-10-minute speed (UDRHP p. 260); ZAP customer support tool active (p. 13).

Target Segment: Whole customer base experiencing order SLA breaches or defective fulfillments.

Operational Mechanism: Trigger-based automated service recovery on failed orders using existing customer support infrastructure (operational safeguard).

Pilot Implementation: Track complaint-to-churn linkage systematically before investing in expensive dedicated proactive service recovery programs.

Expected Output: Early warning operational indicator only.

Key Limitations: The sample may under-represent severe service-driven exits; service recovery is a hygienic operational requirement rather than a differentiated CRM growth engine.

Target KPI: Complaint resolution time; repeat purchase rate after an order complaint Source: Table 19 canonical record (Table 13e, Delivery 4, App 3)
