Stage 1 · Inception & Overview

Customer Switching Behaviour in Indian Quick Commerce

An empirical mixed-methods investigation into customer switching intentions, catalog-driven attrition, and evidence-based CRM strategy in Indian quick commerce. Survey micro-data (N = 184) is triangulated with Zepto's 690-page Updated Draft Red Herring Prospectus (UDRHP-I) regulatory disclosures (E01–E25) and academic Push–Pull–Mooring literature.

⚠️ Methodological Precaution & Epistemological Boundaries
This research adheres strictly to disciplined, non-causal empirical standards. Cross-sectional survey correlations reflect statistical associations among self-reported perceptual measures and do not demonstrate causal mechanisms. Operational metrics from Zepto's regulatory disclosures (UDRHP-I) describe firm-level network capacity and financial constraints without establishing individual customer retention causality. Never-users (n = 60) are non-adopters and remain strictly segregated from former-user switching analyses.

Spearman Rank Correlation (ρ)

Non-parametric monotonic association metric computed across ordinal Likert items. Robust against non-normal skewness. Bootstrapped with 5,000 resamples using a fixed deterministic seed to establish 95% confidence intervals.

Holm-Bonferroni Step-Down

Controls Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER) across the family of 7 correlation tests (H1–H7). Sorts unadjusted p-values in ascending order and adjusts thresholds by p × (8 − rank), preventing spurious alpha inflation.

Mann–Whitney U Non-Parametric Test

Used for two-group medians comparison (H9: Shifted vs Not Shifted; H10: Class A vs Class C). Effect size quantified via rank-biserial correlation r = 1 − (2U / n1n2).

Stage 5 · Actionable Strategy

Evidence-Based CRM Recommendations (R1–R5)

Pragmatic CRM strategic recommendations derived strictly from converged primary survey and regulatory evidence. Evaluated with statistical confidence tiers.

Filter by Status:
R1 · HIGH CONFIDENCE

Serviceability Waitlist & Expansion-Triggered Reactivation

Recommended (Conditional, Testable)

Target Segment: Residents of unserved localities (Class A former users, n = 31; never-users citing coverage, n = 37).

Mechanism: Capture pin codes at registration and on failed-delivery screens. Trigger automated notification when a dark store covering that catchment goes live. Zepto's ₹16,289.75M network expansion (E07) directly enables this strategy.

Primary Limitations: Exit was involuntary; this represents demand recapture rather than true behavioural retention.

KPI: Waitlist-to-first-order conversion; 30/90-day repeat rate Primary Evidence: Table 13d (31/73) & Table 16 (E07)
R2 · MODERATE CONFIDENCE

Restock Alerts & Intelligent Substitution Recommendations

Conditional (Pilot with Control Group)

Target Segment: Current users experiencing repeated failed catalog searches or out-of-stock views.

Mechanism: Log stock-out events and failed searches. Automatically trigger back-in-stock alerts and personalized close-substitute recommendations powered by Zepto's existing in-house ML engine (E18, ₹4,000.00M cloud investment).

Primary Limitations: Associational survey evidence; recommendation engine efficacy must be tested in a randomized holdout pilot.

KPI: Repeat ordering among stock-out affected users vs control Primary Evidence: Table 13e (16/32) & Table 16 (E18)
R3 · LOW-MODERATE CONFIDENCE

Segmented Win-Back for Voluntary Assortment Leavers

Conditional (Small n; Matched Reasons Only)

Target Segment: Former voluntary users whose documented exit reason was product availability or variety (n = 16).

Mechanism: Reason-specific win-back messaging triggered when relevant categories or brands are introduced. Restrict incentives to matched assortment leavers; broad-based discount discounting is unsupported (only 3/32 cited discounts).

Primary Limitations: Small matched segment (n = 16); stated return intention may overstate actual conversion.

KPI: Reactivation rate vs holdout; 30/90-day post-reactivation repeat Primary Evidence: Table 13e (Availability 8, Variety 8)
R4 · CRITICAL RESEARCH FINDING

Switching-Barrier or Habit Lock-In Mechanisms

Not Recommended on Current Evidence

Evaluation Status: NOT RECOMMENDED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE. Switching-barrier evidence is inconclusive and current measurement is problematic.

Scientific Rationale: Hypothesis H5 yielded an anomalous positive coefficient (ρ = +0.721), and familiarity (H6) and inertia (H7) were statistically non-significant. Deploying artificial lock-in or switching barriers without validated reverse-worded re-measurement risks user alienation in a competitive multi-homing market.

Target Segment: All users experiencing delivery SLA failures, defective items, or unresolved app complaints.

Mechanism: Automated micro-reimbursement and priority support queueing immediately following an SLA breach. Track complaint-to-churn linkage before investing in dedicated proactive recovery programs.

Primary Limitations: Sample may under-represent service-driven exits (delivery 4/32, app 3/32); service recovery is a hygienic operational necessity.

KPI: Complaint resolution time; repeat purchase post-complaint Primary Evidence: Table 13e (Delivery 4, App 3)
