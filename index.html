Customer Switching Behaviour in India's Quick-Commerce Industry

An empirical investigation of customer switching intentions, catalog-driven attrition, and CRM strategy in Indian quick commerce: triangulating survey micro-data (N = 184) with Zepto's regulatory disclosures (E01–E25) and academic switching models.

SAMPLE COMPOSITION (N = 184) Cleaned & Verified · Three Lifecycle Partitions
Current Users: n = 51 (27.7%) · Active switching intentions (H1–H7)
Former Users: n = 73 (39.7%) · Realized behavioral exit pathways
Never-Users: n = 60 (32.6%) · Non-adopters (Contextual baseline)
Primary Survey (N = 184) + Robustness Diagnostics (Centering / HTMT) + UDRHP Disclosures (E01–E25) + Academic Literature (L01–L17) + Evidence Triangulation = Evidence-Based CRM Strategy (R1–R5)
⚠️
Methodological Precaution & Epistemological Boundaries: This study adheres strictly to disciplined, non-causal empirical standards. Primary survey correlations represent statistical associations among self-reported perceptual measures and do not establish direct causality. Secondary operational metrics from Zepto's Updated Draft Red Herring Prospectus (UDRHP-I, June 2026) illustrate firm-level capacity and growth trajectories without demonstrating individual consumer retention causality. Never-users (n = 60) are non-adopters and remain strictly excluded from all switching-away analyses.
01

Research Questions & Objectives

Establishing the empirical problem space: separating structural service coverage barriers from voluntary competitor-pull dynamics in quick commerce.

PRIMARY RESEARCH QUESTION

What drives customer switching and multi-homing behaviour away from Zepto toward rival quick-commerce platforms in urban India?

Investigates whether customer attrition is primarily propelled by service dissatisfaction (Push), competitor variety and promotional advantages (Pull), or constrained by perceived switching friction and brand familiarity (Mooring).

FOUR CORE RESEARCH OBJECTIVES
  • RO1: Quantify perceptual Push–Pull–Mooring drivers of switching intention among current users (n = 51).
  • RO2: Disentangle coverage-driven non-serviceability from voluntary competitive attrition among former users (n = 73).
  • RO3: Triangulate survey findings against regulatory disclosures in Zepto's UDRHP-I (690 pages).
  • RO4: Formulate testable, evidence-linked CRM recommendations avoiding speculative lock-in assumptions.

Robustness & Measurement Diagnostics

Methodological stress-testing: response-style sensitivity via within-person centering and discriminant validity bounds via HTMT.

STEP 1
Raw Associations
Strong positive raw correlations observed across Pull factors (ρ ≈ +0.69 to +0.70) and SWEFFORT (ρ = +0.721).
STEP 2
Within-Person Centering
Subtracted respondent mean across Likert items to isolate relative item preference from generalized high scoring.
STEP 3
Correlation Collapse
Pull associations attenuate sharply (ALT ρ drops from +0.685 to −0.03; PROMO CI spans zero [−0.10, +0.55]).
STEP 4
Disciplined Interpretation
Raw correlations are sensitive to response style; switching barriers are inconclusive, requiring non-causal restraint.

CRITICAL ROBUSTNESS CONCLUSION

Switching Barriers Remain Inconclusive, Not Negligible

The positive raw correlation for SWEFFORT (+0.721) contradicts standard PPM theory. Sensitivity testing reveals this collapses to near-zero (+0.05) under within-person centering, alongside high HTMT overlap (0.923) with switching intention.

Core takeaway: This diagnostic indicates measurement overlap and response elevation rather than proof that switching costs are unimportant. Switching barriers must be re-measured with validated multidimensional scales before designing retention interventions around them.

METHODOLOGICAL QUARANTINE

Strict Exclusion from Switching Analytics

Never-users have zero prior consumption history with Zepto. Treating non-adopters as "switchers" would introduce severe category confusion and violate the PPM theoretical premise.

Their data is reported exclusively as market context to quantify unserved geographic demand in urban and peri-urban areas.

Evidence-Based CRM Strategy Recommendations

Testable, conditional CRM interventions directly linked to verified empirical findings, incorporating explicit stop-cards against artificial customer lock-in.

R1 Coverage Demand Capture & Waitlist Notification System
Recommended (Conditional, Testable)
Evidence Base
31/73 former users (42.5%) and 37/60 never-users (61.7%) cite unserved locality. Triangulated with ₹16,289.75M expansion capex (E07).
Customer Problem
Structural non-serviceability forces consumers to shift purchases to available competing quick-commerce platforms.
CRM Mechanism
In-app pincode waitlist & instant push notification when new dark stores launch in previously unserved micro-markets.
Test Design
Track waitlist-to-first-order conversion rates vs. baseline organic adoption in newly launched store catchments.
KPI & Metrics
Waitlist registration volume, notification open rate, 30-day first-order conversion, and 90-day repeat order rate.
Risks & Limitations
Capital-intensive; dark store rollout pace depends on physical real estate acquisition rather than CRM software alone.
R2 Assortment & Stock-Out Recovery Automation
Conditional Pilot (Control Group Required)
Evidence Base
16/32 voluntary former exits (50.0%) cite product availability and variety. 41/68 (60.3%) found items on rivals not on Zepto.
Customer Problem
Unfulfilled search queries and out-of-stock items immediately trigger multi-homing to Blinkit or Swiggy Instamart.
CRM Mechanism
Automated "Back in Stock" alerts, smart substitute recommendations, and search query logging for dark store replenishment.
Test Design
A/B test: In-app substitute suggestions vs. standard "Out of Stock" screen; measure immediate basket retention.
KPI & Metrics
Search exit bounce rate, substitute acceptance rate, and 7-day repeat purchase rate among stocked-out users.
Risks & Limitations
Substitute recommendations may frustrate users if product quality or brand tier differs significantly.
R3 Reason-Matched Win-Back Campaign
Conditional (Small Evidence Base, n=16)
Evidence Base
15/30 voluntary former users expressed willingness to return; voluntary exit sample base is small (n=16 availability/variety exits).
Customer Problem
Generic "We Miss You" discounts fail to address the underlying reason the customer stopped ordering (stock-outs or missing brands).
CRM Mechanism
Trigger personalized win-back messaging only when previously searched missing categories or brands are added to local dark store.
Test Design
Pilot across a single high-density city; compare category-matched win-back vs. generic discount coupon control.
KPI & Metrics
Win-back reactivation rate, 60-day post-reactivation order frequency, and customer acquisition cost vs. lifetime value.
Risks & Limitations
Evidence base is small (n=16); high risk of margin dilution if deployed with blanket discounting.
R4 Switching-Barrier Measurement (NOT Lock-In Friction)
Not Recommended on Current Evidence
Empirical Warning
H5 showed positive raw correlation (+0.721), collapsed to +0.05 post-centering, and exhibited severe HTMT overlap (0.923).
Prohibited Practice
DO NOT DEPLOY ARTIFICIAL LOCK-IN: Introducing artificial friction, non-refundable wallet traps, or cancellation barriers will damage consumer trust.
Required Action Instead
Re-measure switching barriers using multi-dimensional validated scales (procedural, financial, relational) before designing retention interventions around them.
R5 Service Recovery & Fulfillment Failure Monitoring
Monitor Only (Exploratory Baseline)
Evidence Base
Satisfaction was non-significant in predicting switching intention (H1 ρ = +0.170, p=0.249). Minor exit reason among former users (1/32).
Customer Problem
High customer satisfaction does not protect Zepto against multi-homing when rival apps offer better product variety or promotions.
CRM Mechanism
Automated post-failure recovery credit and proactive refund triggers following delayed deliveries or missing items.
KPI & Metrics
Service recovery NPS, repeat purchase within 14 days of an order complaint, and customer support resolution time.
Risks & Limitations
Prevents acute grievance exits but does not resolve structural catalog-driven switching.

Final Synthesis & Claims Ledger

Summary answers to what this study empirical establishes, what requires cautious interpretation, and what should NOT be claimed.

✓

Empirically Supported Findings

1. Customer switching in quick commerce is multidimensional: coverage constraints and catalog variety dominate over service dissatisfaction.

2. Former-user attrition is split between geographic non-serviceability (42.5%) and voluntary competitive exit (43.8%).

3. Among voluntary exits, product availability (25.0%) and assortment variety (25.0%) account for 50.0% of realized departures.

4. 60.3% of former users confirmed competitors carried products/variants not easily found on Zepto.

◐

Findings Requiring Cautious Methodological Interpretation

1. Strong raw correlations for Pull factors (ALT, VAR, PROMO ≈ +0.69 to +0.70) attenuate substantially post-centering, indicating response-style elevation.

2. Switching effort (SWEFFORT) is inconclusive due to opposite-sign raw correlation (+0.721) and high HTMT overlap (0.923) with switching intention.

3. Secondary regulatory metrics (UDRHP-I) illustrate firm-level expansion and cohort retention but do not establish individual consumer causality.

✕

What Should NOT Be Claimed

1. Do NOT claim that product stock-outs or promotions caused customer switching (cross-sectional survey data cannot establish causality).

2. Do NOT treat never-users (n = 60) as churned switchers (they are geographic non-adopters with zero consumption history).

3. Do NOT claim that switching barriers are unimportant based on the H5 anomaly (they require multidimensional re-measurement).

4. Do NOT recommend artificial friction, wallet lock-in, or deceptive retention hurdles.
